\begin{abstract}
Reliable defenses against data poisoning require evidence that attack information survives each stage between a network payload and a detector decision. This study introduces an audit of a controlled topological data analysis (TDA) pipeline on the UNSW-NB15 Payload-Byte dataset. Across five seeds, 50.80 \% of repeated final-vector members first coincide as raw payloads, 46.68 \% first coincide at the single binary threshold, and only 2.52 \% first coincide during persistent homology; no later stage
creates an exact merger. An analysis-locked comparison then changes only the representation, replacing one threshold with a fixed nine-threshold stack for four methods of byte permutations.
The stack reduces poisoned rows that exactly match a clean feature vector from 2.60–4.32 \% to 0.16–0.28 \%, a 92.0–95.9 \% reduction, confirming that the single-cutoff equality mechanism is real and closable. That gain does not improve poison removal at matched clean-data cost. Absolute removal is low in both arms—0.00–0.20\% for the single threshold and 0.00–0.64\% for the stack at cluster-purity budgets of 0.80 and above. Eliminating exact coordinate collisions therefore exposes density-based cluster formation, rather than coordinate equality, as the binding downstream limitation, and yields a reproducible evaluation standard.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Machine-learning network intrusion detection systems (NIDS) learn patterns
from historical traffic, making the integrity of training data a security
requirement rather than a routine data-quality assumption. A poisoning
adversary can corrupt that data so that a learned detector acquires an
attacker-chosen weakness or simply becomes less reliable. This threat belongs
to the broader adversarial-machine-learning problem described by Biggio and
Roli~\cite{biggio2018}. It is especially relevant in network defense, where
traffic is high-dimensional, labels are expensive, and model retraining may
repeat automatically.

Monkam, De Lucia, and Bastian recently proposed a TDA-based method that maps
packet payloads to persistence-derived feature vectors and applies unsupervised
clustering to identify poisoned observations~\cite{monkam2024}. TDA is an
appealing basis for this task because persistent homology summarizes connected
components and loops across a filtration instead of relying directly on every
pixel or byte~\cite{carlsson2009,cohensteiner2007}. Yet that compression also
creates an interpretive problem: when a poisoned packet is not isolated, the
responsible stage could be the input frame, a lossy image representation, the
topological summary, or the clustering geometry. Reporting only final capture
cannot distinguish these mechanisms.

This paper asks: \emph{Where does information needed to distinguish
byte-permutation poisoning disappear in a controlled reconstruction of a
TDA-based network-traffic detector, and does eliminating single-threshold
representation collisions improve downstream poison removal?} The study makes
three contributions. First, it defines a mutually exclusive
\emph{earliest-merger} audit that attributes every repeated final-vector member
to the first pipeline stage at which exact equality appears. Second, it tests a
single prespecified representation intervention---a nine-threshold stack---
while holding the attack realizations, raster, topological features, and OPTICS
settings fixed. Third, it separates three claims that are often treated as one:
whether an algebraic blind spot is repaired, whether poisoned samples become
unusual relative to clean traffic, and whether downstream removal improves at
the same clean-data cost.

The experiments localize 97.48\% of repeated-member collision mass to the raw
payload or binary mask and reduce exact clean/poison coordinate matches by
92.0--95.9\%. Neither representation, however, removes an appreciable share of
poison at strict clean-data cost. That outcome relocates the limiting
mechanism from coordinate equality to density-based cluster formation, which
sharpens rather than obscures the downstream conclusion. These conditional
findings concern four fixed permutation families, the packet-level
Payload-Byte derivative, and a controlled reconstruction rather than an exact
reproduction of Ref.~\cite{monkam2024}.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\subsection{Payload-based intrusion detection and poisoning}
UNSW-NB15 is a widely used intrusion-detection benchmark containing normal
traffic and nine attack categories~\cite{moustafa2015}. This work uses only the
packet-level Payload-Byte derivative produced by the extraction and labeling
procedure of Farrukh \emph{et al.}~\cite{farrukh2022}, not the original
flow-feature table. Each observation contains 1,500 padded payload-byte fields
and metadata. This distinction matters: exact equality of two padded packet
payloads is not the same object as equality of two original UNSW-NB15 flow
records.

Poisoning evaluations must distinguish an attack transformation from its
realized effect. A permutation acting on padding, equal values, or bytes in the
same bin can leave the next pipeline stage unchanged. The main comparison
therefore redraws attack parameters until every poisoned row differs from its
source at the raw-byte level.

\subsection{Persistent homology as a machine-learning representation}
For a scalar-valued filtration on an image, persistent homology records the
birth and death of topological features as sublevel sets grow. A persistence
diagram is a multiset of points $(b,d)$, and vector summaries make these
diagrams usable by conventional learning algorithms. Stability results explain
why persistence can suppress small perturbations~\cite{cohensteiner2007}; that
property is valuable for noise tolerance but is not injectivity. Distinct
images may have the same persistence diagram, and distinct diagrams may have
the same finite summary vector. The image, cubical-persistence,
and summary operations are implemented with
\texttt{giotto-tda}~\cite{tauzin2021}.

The downstream clusterer is OPTICS, which orders observations by density and
can designate sparse observations as noise~\cite{ankerst1999}. Because clean
and poisoned samples can both be unclustered, poison purity is used only for
retrospective scoring, not as a deployable cluster-labeling rule.

Prior work establishes the feasibility of a TDA--clustering defense on network
payloads~\cite{monkam2024}. The present study instead audits information flow
through a fixed reconstruction and conducts one controlled repair. This design
identifies the mechanism removed by the intervention and the downstream
mechanism that then limits performance.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Controlled reconstruction and experimental frames}
For each seed in $\{42,123,456,789,1024\}$, the experiment draws the same
5,000-row sample from the Payload-Byte table and appends 500 poisoned variants
(10\%). Hereafter, \emph{clean} means an unmodified row in the experimental
frame, not necessarily benign network traffic; poisoned sources are selected
from rows bearing an attack label in the dataset. All results are means and
population standard deviations over these five seeds unless stated otherwise.

Two frames are used, and results do not transfer between them.
\emph{Frame~A} is the legacy 60-transposition frame that carries the
provenance audit; it accepts the attack realization as drawn, so some poisoned
rows are byte-identical to their sources. \emph{Frame~B} is the
common-eligibility substrate that carries the control--repair comparison;
attack parameters are redrawn until every poisoned row differs from its source
at the raw-byte level. Because Frame~B eliminates raw no-ops by construction,
the Frame~A attribution of collision mass is a statement about Frame~A alone.

Payload arrays are zero-padded to 1,500 bytes. Because zero may also be a valid
payload byte, conservative support is defined as one position beyond the last
nonzero byte. It may omit legitimate trailing zeros but cannot include appended
padding. In Frame~B, eligible sources have at least 120 supported bytes and a
shared, deterministic attackability witness. The four prespecified families are:
(i) 60 disjoint transpositions; (ii) reversal of one 120-byte block; (iii) a
swap of two disjoint 60-byte blocks; and (iv) a nonzero cyclic shift of the
supported prefix. All act entirely inside conservative support. In Frame~B all
20 family--seed cells therefore contain exactly 500 raw-changing poisoned
rows.

The legacy control reshapes each payload into a $30\times50$ image and applies
a fitted binary threshold $t=0.4$. Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows this arm
beside the intervention defined in Section~\ref{sec:stack}; the two differ
only in binarization. The fitted maximum is 255, so the effective
byte cut is 102. Five scalar filtrations follow: height directions $(0,1)$ and
$(1,0)$, and radial centers $(0,50)$, $(0,25)$, and $(30,0)$. Each branch uses
cubical persistence, diagram scaling, persistence entropy, and five amplitude
summaries (bottleneck, Wasserstein, landscape, Betti, and heat) in homology
dimensions 0 and 1. The installed implementation produces 12 features per
filtration and a 60-dimensional concatenation. OPTICS is fixed at
\texttt{min\_samples}$=5$ and \texttt{max\_eps}$=2.0$ with its standard
$\xi$-based extraction. No thresholds, block weights, filtrations, summaries,
or clustering settings were selected after results were observed.

\subsection{Stage-wise collision provenance (Frame~A)}
Before the repair comparison, the provenance audit is applied to Frame~A, the
standing five-seed legacy control with 60-transposition poisoning.
For every row, an exact canonical signature is computed at the raw payload,
support record, binary mask, five filtration images, unscaled persistence
diagrams, scaled diagrams, 12-feature branch summaries, and final vector.
Diagram points are sorted by homology dimension, birth, and death after
excluding diagonal padding; no float is rounded before equality is declared.
Because each stage is a deterministic function of the preceding stage,
equality can be created but not destroyed. A repeated final-vector member is
assigned to the earliest stage at which its exact equivalence class first ceases
to be a singleton. The resulting categories are mutually exclusive and sum to
the final repeated-member collision mass. All five runs satisfy the required
class-refinement monotonicity check.

This denominator is important. A reported share such as 50.80\% means 50.80\%
of \emph{rows belonging to repeated final-vector classes}; it is not the
duplicate rate among all 5,500 rows. The analysis separately reports the
\emph{coordinate-class obstruction}: poisoned rows sharing an exact final
feature vector with at least one clean row, divided by all poisoned rows.

The targeted representation weakness follows directly from the permutation
action. Let $P_\sigma$ permute pixel positions and let
$B_t(\vect{x})_j=\mathbf{1}\{x_j>255t\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
 B_t(P_\sigma\vect{x})=P_\sigma B_t(\vect{x}), \qquad
 \|B_t(P_\sigma\vect{x})\|_1=\|B_t(\vect{x})\|_1 .
 \label{eq:invariance}
\end{equation}
The cut $255t$ is fixed because a permutation preserves the byte multiset and
therefore fixes the fitted maximum, which is 255 on this data. The foreground
count is invariant, and if every moved byte remains in its old threshold bin,
the entire binary image is unchanged. Position-dependent filtrations can
respond when the mask changes, so~\eqref{eq:invariance} does not imply that all
topological summaries are permutation-invariant. That question is measured by
the downstream signatures rather than assumed.

\subsection{Fixed multithreshold intervention}
\label{sec:stack}
The only intervention replaces $t=0.4$ with the fixed stack
$T=\{0.1,0.2,\ldots,0.9\}$. For every threshold, the unchanged pipeline emits a
60-vector $\vect{f}_t(\vect{x})$; the repair representation is
\begin{equation}
 \vect{F}(\vect{x})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{9}}
 [\vect{f}_{0.1}(\vect{x}),\ldots,\vect{f}_{0.9}(\vect{x})]\in\mathbb{R}^{540}.
 \label{eq:stack}
\end{equation}
The fixed factor $1/\sqrt{9}$ preserves Euclidean distance scale if all nine
blocks were identical, preventing dimensionality alone from silently changing
the meaning of \texttt{max\_eps}. Blocks receive equal, nonlearned weights. The
control is the $t=0.4$ block extracted from the same fitted comparison and the
same poisoned realization; exact equality with the legacy 60-vector is a
regression requirement.

\subsection{Evaluation endpoints}
For a retrospective poison-purity cutoff
$q\in\{1.0,>0.95,>0.90,>0.80,>0.50\}$, all non-noise clusters meeting $q$ are
marked for removal. Let $R_p(q)$ be the fraction of poison removed and $R_c(q)$
the fraction of clean rows removed. The prespecified primary comparison at
each control point $q$ is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(q)=\max_{q':\,R_c^{\rm stack}(q')\le R_c^{\rm ctl}(q)}
 R_p^{\rm stack}(q')-R_p^{\rm ctl}(q).
 \label{eq:matchedcost}
\end{equation}
This matched-clean-cost endpoint prevents apparent recall gains purchased by
discarding more unpoisoned data. Secondary outcomes include exact feature
matches with clean, clean and poison noise fractions, cluster count, and
strict-purity removal.

\subsection{Reproducibility and analysis lock}
The dataset, seeds, attacks, thresholds, block weights, filtrations, summaries,
OPTICS parameters, purity cutoffs, and matched-cost endpoint were fixed before
the full comparison. No component was selected or tuned after observing the
results. Each family--seed record verifies 5,000 unmodified and 500 appended
rows, zero raw no-ops, 60 control features, 540 stack features, finite outputs,
and all removal points. The extracted $t=0.4$ block must exactly equal the
legacy output, the reconstructed provenance matrix must be bitwise identical
to production output, and equivalence classes must refine monotonically across
stages. Artifacts are saved by seed and summarized with population standard
deviations.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Frame~A: the audit localizes exact collisions upstream}
In the five-seed Frame~A provenance audit, the final 60-vector
repeated-member fraction is
$\meanstd{56.33}{0.68}$\%. Fig.~\ref{fig:provenance} partitions that mass by its
first merger. Raw-identical padded payloads account for
$\meanstd{50.80}{0.50}$\%, and threshold-0.4 binarization accounts for another
$\meanstd{46.68}{0.46}$\%. Only $\meanstd{2.52}{0.31}$\% first merges in
unscaled cubical persistence. The support record, filtration images, Scaler,
vector summaries, and final concatenation add zero earliest mergers in every
seed. The support record is bijective with the padded payload, and all seeds
contain zero all-zero payload rows; raw repetition is therefore not an
empty-packet artifact.

The audit also separates equality from density behavior. Under the fixed
control, $\meanstd{42.28}{1.64}$\% of poison shares an exact final coordinate
with clean, while $\meanstd{56.24}{1.65}$\% is labeled OPTICS noise. Only
$\meanstd{1.80}{0.51}$\% is removed in a 100\%-poison cluster. Exact collisions
are therefore load-bearing under this fit, but the noise assignment affects a
larger portion of poisoned rows.

The strict-purity failure decomposition is exhaustive over all poisoned rows.
Averaged over seeds, 39.48\% of poison is non-noise but shares a final vector
with a clean row, 2.48\% is non-noise and coordinate-distinct but lies in a
mixed cluster, and the residual category is exactly zero in every seed. Exact
coordinate sharing thus constrains this fit without being treated as a
universal capture ceiling.

\subsection{Frame~B: multithresholding repairs the representation}
The control--repair comparison uses Frame~B, so its control is the matched
single-threshold arm on that substrate rather than the Frame~A provenance
control. The primary representation gain is a sharp reduction in poisoned rows
that exactly match any clean feature vector (Fig.~\ref{fig:repair}). The
reduction is consistent across all four families and all five seeds: family
means fall from 2.60--4.32\% under the control to 0.16--0.28\% under the
repair, a 92.0--95.9\% reduction. In absolute terms this is a fall from 13--24
of 500 poisoned rows to 0--2 of 500; because the repair counts reach zero in
8 of 20 cells, the reduction is reported as a percentage rather than as a
ratio. The intervention therefore succeeds at its prespecified representation
objective.

\subsection{Frame~B: detection does not follow, and both arms sit near the
floor}
Table~\ref{tab:matched} reports absolute poison removal for both arms
alongside the matched-cost difference. At every purity budget of 0.80 and
above, the single-threshold control removes 0.00--0.20\% of poison and the
stack removes 0.00--0.64\%. Both nonzero strict-budget differences rest on a
minority of seeds: block reversal's $\meanstd{+0.64}{0.53}$ points comes from
a single poison-only cluster of about five packets in three of five seeds, and
cyclic shift's $\meanstd{-0.20}{0.40}$ points from one seed. Transpositions and
block swap are identically zero in every seed. At the loosest budget, $>0.50$,
the control reaches 1.24--1.88\% while the stack stays at 0.00--0.64\%, so
every family is then worse than control.

Clean false-removal is 0.0000 for the stack at every budget and for the
control at every budget of 0.80 and above. The matched-cost constraint
in~\eqref{eq:matchedcost} is therefore inactive through $q>0.80$ and binds
only at $q>0.50$, where the control incurs a clean cost of 0.06--0.11\%.

The downstream geometry accounts for this floor. From control to stack, poison
noise rises from 85.2--87.5\% to 98.7--99.3\%, clean noise rises from about
37.1\% to 54.5--54.6\%, and mean cluster count falls from 139.6--142.4 to
107.6--108.8. The stack separates coordinates but fragments the population.

Because both arms sit near zero at operational budgets, this comparison should
not be read as a repaired representation failing to improve a working
detector. Neither representation supports meaningful poison removal under the
fixed clustering configuration. Eliminating exact coordinate collisions
therefore does not resolve the limitation; it exposes density-based cluster
formation, rather than coordinate equality, as the binding constraint.

An exploratory single-seed diagnostic is consistent with this reading. On 200
clean/source pairs per family at seed 42, the median attack displacement is
10.7--17.6\% of the median displacement between arbitrary clean pairs under
the control and 7.0--12.2\% under the stack, and no attack displacement in
either representation exceeds the clean-pair 95th percentile. The ratio falls
because the stack shrinks attack displacement---from 11.61 to 8.54 for
transpositions, for instance---while raising the clean-pair reference from
66.1 to 70.3. Two limits apply. This reference is a population-scale pairing
rather than the local $k$-nearest-neighbor scale that governs OPTICS
reachability, so it cannot by itself explain clusterability, and it is
measured at one seed. It is reported as indicative context, not as part of the
five-seed evidence.

\section{Discussion}
The main gain from the experimentation is a three-level evaluation framework.
At the \emph{representation} level, the fixed stack closes the targeted
single-threshold equality mechanism. At the \emph{statistical} level,
attack-induced displacement can be compared with ordinary clean variation. At
the \emph{detector} level, removal can be assessed at a fixed clean-data cost
and against the absolute rate the arms actually achieve. This ordering shows
where an intervention succeeds and prevents success at one level from being
reported as success at the next.

The provenance audit localizes 97.48\% of repeated-member collision mass to the
raw data or single-threshold mask. The small diagram-stage component creates no
new clean/poison mixed classes, and later summaries add no exact mergers. This
does not establish that TDA is injective; it shows that exact confusion in
Frame~A is primarily created upstream of the topological feature map.

The stack adds distinctions, but equal weighting does not make them
attack-specific. In the exploratory diagnostic, median attack displacement in
fact falls under the stack while the clean-pair reference rises, and with the
density radius held fixed
the 540-dimensional population becomes less clusterable: nearly all poisoned
rows and more than half of clean rows are assigned to noise. Collision
prevalence is therefore a poor proxy for usable poison separation.

The most consequential reading is negative but specific. Because control
removal is already 0.00--0.20\% at operational budgets, this experiment cannot
show that repairing collisions helps a functioning detector, and it does not
claim to. What it does show is that coordinate equality was not the operative
obstacle: removing 92.0--95.9\% of exact matches leaves removal at the floor
in every family, while the noise fractions move sharply. The binding constraint under
this configuration is whether OPTICS forms poison-dominant clusters at all.

Accordingly, a defense claim should report a representation endpoint, a
separation endpoint, and an operational endpoint stated in absolute terms with
its clean-data cost. A large reduction in collisions alone establishes neither
density support nor deployable poison capture.

The claims are limited to a padded packet-level derivative, one recoverable
pipeline reconstruction, five seeds, four structured permutation families, and
a single fixed clustering configuration; a different clusterer or radius could
change the detector-level conclusion. They do not establish cross-dataset
generalization, and retrospective cluster purity is not deployable. Later work
should fix its endpoints in advance and test label-free scores, metrics
calibrated to local clean geometry, and a second dataset rather than tune the
present experiment post hoc.

\section{Conclusion}
Three conclusions follow from the controlled experiments.

(1) The stage-wise audit localizes 97.48\% of repeated-member collision mass in
Frame~A to raw payload identity or the single-threshold mask, before persistent
homology; the five filtration images, the persistence summaries, and the final
concatenation create no exact mergers in any seed.

(2) The fixed nine-threshold stack achieves its targeted representation
objective, reducing exact clean/poison feature matches by 92.0--95.9\% across
four attack families and five seeds. The single-cutoff equality mechanism is
therefore real and closable.

(3) Closing it does not improve poison removal at matched clean-data cost, and
neither arm removes an appreciable share of poison: 0.00--0.20\% for the
control and 0.00--0.64\% for the stack at purity budgets of 0.80 and above,
with poisoned rows assigned to OPTICS noise rising from 85--87\% to about
99\%. Coordinate equality was not the operative obstacle; density-based
cluster formation is.

Together these results give a reproducible standard for evaluating poisoning
defenses: localize exact collisions, verify the intended repair, measure
separation against clean variation, and require operational gains to be
reported in absolute terms at a fixed clean-data cost.
