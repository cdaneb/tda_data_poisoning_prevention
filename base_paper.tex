\begin{abstract}
This study audits a topological data analysis (TDA) poisoning detector using
UNSW-NB15 and CICIDS2017 Payload-Byte data. Author-supplied artifacts recover a
$1\times1500$, threshold-0.3 feature map, but omit most poison examples and
three selected clustering configurations. Half of the supplied 280 features
are constant and 74.069\% of rows belong to repeated feature classes. Separate
batch fitting changes every available poison vector but not DBSCAN assignments.
In a controlled $30\times50$ reconstruction, 97.48\% of repeated-vector
collision mass originates in raw payloads or single-threshold binarization. A
fixed nine-threshold stack removes 92.0--95.9\% of exact poison--unmodified
matches, yet fixed OPTICS removal remains near zero. Clean-calibrated Isolation Forest
and kNN discovery scores instead capture 14.02--18.95\% near a 5\%
unmodified-removal budget. An analysis-locked 90-cell confirmation retains
7.01--14.73\% capture, but finds no general 540-feature advantage across the
unseen-seed UNSW and both matched and scale-arm CICIDS populations. Discovery
capture is 0.29--3.42\% at nominal 0.1--1\% budgets, and confirmation capture
is 1.46--4.05\% at 1\%. Representation repair, cluster separation, and
operational generalization are distinct claims.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Training-data integrity is a security requirement for machine-learning network
intrusion detection systems. A poisoning adversary can corrupt historical
traffic so that a learned detector acquires an attacker-chosen weakness
~\cite{biggio2018}.

Monkam, De Lucia, and Bastian proposed mapping packet payloads to
persistence-derived features and clustering them before model
training~\cite{monkam2024}. Persistent homology compresses connected
components and loops across filtrations~\cite{carlsson2009,cohensteiner2007},
but failed capture can arise from the input data, binarization, topological
summary, or cluster geometry. A final score alone cannot identify the cause.

This paper therefore asks what the supplied implementation reproduces, where
byte-permutation information disappears, and whether surviving signal supports
label-free removal at a stated clean-data cost. It contributes: (i) a forensic
audit of supplied 126/280-feature artifacts; (ii) an earliest-merger audit and
fixed nine-threshold repair; (iii) duplicate-group-safe classifier evaluation;
and (iv) clean-calibrated novelty detection with independently locked UNSW and
CICIDS confirmation. The supplied DoS examples and the controlled permutation
experiments remain separate threat models, and neither is presented as an exact
reproduction of Ref.~\cite{monkam2024}.

\section{Background and Related Work}
\subsection{Dataset and supplied artifacts}
UNSW-NB15 is a widely used intrusion-detection benchmark containing normal
traffic and nine attack categories~\cite{moustafa2015}. This work uses only the
packet-level Payload-Byte derivative produced by the extraction and labeling
procedure of Farrukh \emph{et al.}~\cite{farrukh2022}: 79,881 padded
1,500-byte packet records, not the original flow-feature table. Confirmation
ingests a pre-existing 1,410,255-row CICIDS2017 CSV~\cite{sharafaldin2018} in
the same 1,500-byte Payload-Byte schema~\cite{farrukh2022}. This study did not
regenerate that derivative from packet captures; its exact extraction
provenance beyond the schema and frozen file hash is therefore unverified.

The supplied package contains six notebooks, nineteen CSV files, two feature
workbooks, and an archive. Its poisoning path uses a $1\times1500$ raster,
usually threshold 0.3, and 72/126 features; Algorithm~1 instead states 0.4.
Only 18 of 1,000 saved poison examples and only the DBSCAN configuration are
available. The generator and selected HDBSCAN, OPTICS, and Mean Shift settings
are missing.

\subsection{Representation and evaluation}
Persistence diagrams record feature births and deaths; finite summaries enable
machine learning but are not injective~\cite{cohensteiner2007,tauzin2021}.
Raster geometry and fitted preprocessing also matter. A $1\times1500$ cubical
complex has no nontrivial $H_1$ loops. Moreover, \texttt{Binarizer} learns a
batch maximum and \texttt{Scaler} a diagram scale, so separate batch fits can
create incompatible coordinates.

Exact raw payloads and TDA vectors also repeat. Random row splits can place one
equivalence class in both train and test; group-safe splits prevent this.
Finally, OPTICS may label both poison and unmodified rows as noise
~\cite{ankerst1999}. Cluster purity is therefore retrospective. Operational
evaluation instead fits and calibrates novelty scores on disjoint trusted
unmodified subsets and reports poison capture with realized held-out
unmodified removal.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Forensic and faithful-representation audits}
Files, notebook execution, sample construction, fit boundaries, and clustering
calls were inventoried. Workbooks were checked for constant coordinates, exact
classes, label conflicts, and raw alignment. The recovered 126 map uses seven
filtrations, $H_0/H_1$, and nine summaries on a $1\times1500$ raster; the
280-feature cell uses 20 summaries without diagram scaling. Synthetic diagrams
verified feature order. The 280 workbook aligns to raw rows, whereas the
31,000-row 126 workbook does not because 10,000 rows were sampled without a
seed.

A locked seed-60 diagnostic then changes only the 126-map fit protocol:
separate poison/unmodified fits versus one combined fit. It uses all 18
delivered poisons, 378 normal and 180 unmodified attack-category rows, a fixed
50\% split, threshold 0.3, and DBSCAN (\texttt{eps}$=200$,
\texttt{min\_samples}$=9$). This preserves the notebook's $21{:}10$
unmodified composition and 3.125\% poison share. Learned cuts/scales, feature
displacement, silhouette, ARI, composition, and removal are recorded.
Multi-seed expansion requires both state divergence and a material cluster
change.

\subsection{Controlled reconstruction and experimental frames}
Each seed in $\{42,123,456,789,1024\}$ uses 5,000 unmodified rows plus 500
poisoned variants. Here \emph{unmodified} is not necessarily benign; poison
sources carry attack labels. Frame~A audits the standing 60-transposition
pipeline and permits raw no-ops. Frame~B redraws parameters until every poison
changes its source, so results do not transfer between frames.

Frame~B defines conservative support as one position beyond the last nonzero
byte; attacks act only inside it. They are 60 disjoint transpositions, one
120-byte reversal, two swapped 60-byte blocks, or a nonzero cyclic shift.
Each of 20 family--seed cells contains 500 raw-changing poisons.

The controlled reconstruction reshapes payloads to $30\times50$, binarizes at
$t=0.4$ (byte cut 102), and applies two height and three radial filtrations.
Cubical persistence, diagram scaling, entropy, and five amplitudes over
$H_0/H_1$ produce 60 features. OPTICS uses
\texttt{min\_samples}$=5$, \texttt{max\_eps}$=2.0$, and standard
$\xi$ extraction. All settings were fixed before evaluation.

\subsection{Stage-wise collision provenance (Frame~A)}
Exact signatures are computed at raw payload, support record, binary mask,
filtrations, unscaled/scaled diagrams, summaries, and final vector. Diagram
points are canonically ordered without float rounding. Each repeated final
member is assigned to its first nonsingleton equivalence class. Categories are
mutually exclusive, and class-refinement monotonicity holds in every seed.
Shares use repeated final-vector members as denominator, not all 5,500 rows.
The separate coordinate obstruction is the fraction of poison exactly matching
any unmodified final vector.

If $\phi_k$ is the signature at stage $k$, define
\begin{equation}
 i\sim_k j \iff \phi_k(x_i)=\phi_k(x_j),\qquad
 e(i)=\min\{k:|[i]_{\sim_k}|>1\}.
 \label{eq:earliest}
\end{equation}
For the set $M$ of repeated final-vector members, the stage-$k$ attribution is
$|\{i\in M:e(i)=k\}|/|M|$. This construction makes the reported percentages a
partition of collision mass rather than overlapping duplicate counts.

The targeted representation weakness follows directly from the permutation
action. Let $P_\sigma$ permute pixel positions and let
$B_t(\vect{x})_j=\mathbf{1}\{x_j>255t\}$. Then
\begin{equation}
 B_t(P_\sigma\vect{x})=P_\sigma B_t(\vect{x}), \qquad
 \|B_t(P_\sigma\vect{x})\|_1=\|B_t(\vect{x})\|_1 .
 \label{eq:invariance}
\end{equation}
Permutation preserves the fitted maximum and foreground count. If moved bytes
remain within threshold classes, the whole mask is unchanged. Equation
~\eqref{eq:invariance} is equivariance, not invariance of later
position-dependent filtrations.

\subsection{Fixed multithreshold intervention}
\label{sec:stack}
The only intervention replaces $t=0.4$ with the fixed stack
$T=\{0.1,0.2,\ldots,0.9\}$. For every threshold, the unchanged pipeline emits a
60-vector $\vect{f}_t(\vect{x})$; the repair representation is
\begin{equation}
 \vect{F}(\vect{x})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{9}}
 [\vect{f}_{0.1}(\vect{x}),\ldots,\vect{f}_{0.9}(\vect{x})]\in\mathbb{R}^{540}.
 \label{eq:stack}
\end{equation}
The fixed factor $1/\sqrt{9}$ preserves Euclidean distance scale if all nine
blocks were identical. Blocks have equal, fixed weights; the control is the
$t=0.4$ block from the same poison realization and must exactly match the
production 60-vector.

\subsection{Evaluation protocols}
Retrospective OPTICS removal selects non-noise clusters above poison-purity
cutoffs $q\in\{1.0,>0.95,>0.90,>0.80,>0.50\}$. Let
$R_p^a(q)$ and $R_c^a(q)$ be the fractions of poison and unmodified rows
removed in arm $a$. At each control cutoff, the matched-unmodified-cost
difference is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta(q)=
 \max_{\substack{q':\,R_c^{\mathrm{stk}}(q')\\
                  \le R_c^{\mathrm{ctl}}(q)}}
 \left[R_p^{\mathrm{stk}}(q')-R_p^{\mathrm{ctl}}(q)\right].
 \label{eq:matched}
\end{equation}
Thus the stack receives no more unmodified-removal cost than the control;
exact matching and noise rates are secondary endpoints.

On all 79,881 aligned rows, five-seed 100-tree random forests compare raw bytes
with TDA-280 for ten-class (20\% test) and binary (10\% test) tasks. Random-row
splits are paired with raw- and TDA-identity group splits; conflicting labels
remain. The model seed equals the split seed.

For label-free detection, Frame~B unmodified rows are split by raw identity
into 60\% training, 20\% calibration, and 20\% evaluation. Training-only
standardization precedes 10-neighbor distance, 20-neighbor novelty LOF,
200-tree Isolation Forest, and RBF one-class SVM ($\nu=0.01$). Calibration
quantiles set nominal $0.1,0.5,1,2,5\%$ removal budgets. No poison label fits a
detector or threshold; capture is reported with realized held-out unmodified
removal, precision, AUROC, and AUPRC.

Formally, let $\mathcal C$, $\mathcal U$, and $\mathcal P$ denote calibration
unmodified, held-out unmodified, and poisoned sets, and let larger $s(x)$ mean
more anomalous. For nominal budget $b$, the calibration-only threshold and
reported operating point are
\begin{align}
 \tau_b &= Q_{1-b}\{s(x):x\in\mathcal C\}, \nonumber\\
 C_p(b) &= |\{x\in\mathcal P:s(x)>\tau_b\}|/|\mathcal P|, \nonumber\\
 C_u(b) &= |\{x\in\mathcal U:s(x)>\tau_b\}|/|\mathcal U|.
 \label{eq:novelty}
\end{align}
$C_p$ is poison capture and $C_u$ is realized unmodified removal. Reporting
both prevents a calibration-budget target from being mistaken for its
held-out cost.

After discovery was sealed, an independent confirmation fixed Isolation Forest
as primary, kNN as secondary, seeds 2026--2030, and nominal 1\% and 5\%
budgets. The unseen-seed UNSW and matched-CICIDS arms repeat all four families
with 5,000 unmodified and 500 poisoned rows; the CICIDS scale arm uses 50,000
unmodified and 5,000 transposition-poisoned rows. Its locked criteria are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(P1)] unseen-seed UNSW mean paired IF-540-minus-IF-60 capture is positive;
\item[(P2)] its paired 95\% bootstrap interval excludes zero;
\item[(P3)] unseen-seed UNSW mean IF-540 capture is at least 12\%;
\item[(P4)] unseen-seed UNSW mean IF-540 unmodified removal is 4--6\%;
\item[(T1)] matched-CICIDS mean IF-540 unmodified removal is 4--6\%;
\item[(T2)] matched-CICIDS mean paired IF-540-minus-IF-60 capture is positive;
\item[(T3)] CICIDS-scale mean IF-540 unmodified removal is 4--6\%.
\end{enumerate}
kNN carries no separate pass/fail criterion and cannot rescue a failed primary
claim; it is reported as locked secondary evidence.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
Each study has an immutable specification and machine-readable artifacts.
Gates verify sample counts, zero Frame~B raw no-ops, finite 60/540 features,
production-block equality, provenance monotonicity, and zero group overlap.
Settings and endpoints were fixed before each full run. The confirmation adds
literal row/index hashes, clean-only fit/calibration checks, attack-parent
reproduction, finite score checks, and atomic cell completeness; all 90 cells
pass. A cell is one population--family--seed--representation combination:
$4\times5\times2=40$ for unseen-seed UNSW, 40 for matched CICIDS, and
$1\times5\times2=10$ for CICIDS scale. Both detectors and budgets are evaluated
within each cell.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Supplied-material findings}
The recovered notebook path uses $1\times1500$, threshold 0.3, and 126
features. Its so-called benign set is 21,000 normal plus 10,000 unmodified
attack-category rows sampled without a seed; 1,000 poison rows would be 3.125\%
of the combined set. Only 18 poisons from 11 DoS parents were delivered; all
change their parents, but none preserves the byte multiset like the controlled
attacks. No composition accompanies the saved DBSCAN silhouette (0.887854),
and missing poison data, generation code, and three cluster settings prevent
exact reproduction.

The aligned TDA-280 workbook has 140 constant coordinates, 24,925 unique
vectors, and 59,167 repeated members (74.069\%). Raw duplicates account for
80.587\% of those members; conflicting-label membership rises from 14.376\%
raw to 28.332\% after TDA. Intermediate states are unavailable, so the 19.413\%
post-raw share cannot be assigned to binarization versus persistence. The 126
workbook has 63 constant coordinates and 85.387\% repeated members.
Alternating $H_1$ coordinates are constant in both $1\times1500$ maps.

Separate fitting learns byte cuts 76.5 and 36.6 and changes all 18 poison
vectors (mean $L_2=531.500$), but DBSCAN labels are identical (ARI 1.0).
Both arms place all nine training poisons in the same 249-row mixed cluster.
Separate/shared silhouettes are 0.920/0.879; both remove zero poison. Thus the
fit confound is real at representation level but unconfirmed at decision level,
and the multi-seed gate remains closed.

Duplicate-safe classifier evaluation also changes interpretation. For TDA-280,
final-vector grouping lowers multiclass accuracy from 0.7958 to 0.7293 and
binary accuracy from 0.9581 to 0.8688. Raw bytes outperform TDA-280 under every
split protocol; their corresponding grouped accuracies are 0.8030 and 0.9080.
Large conflicting-label equivalence classes also increase between-seed
variation.

\subsection{Frame~B: multithresholding repairs the representation}
On Frame~B, exact poison--unmodified matches fall from family means
2.60--4.32\% to 0.16--0.28\% (Fig.~\ref{fig:repair}), a 92.0--95.9\%
reduction across every family and seed. The stack therefore repairs the
targeted equality mechanism.

\subsection{Discovery scores recover signal that fixed OPTICS discards}
All 40 discovery cells pass analysis-locked gates. At the nominal 5\% budget,
kNN captures 14.02\% of poison at 4.83\% realized unmodified removal with 60
features and 17.79\% at 5.23\% with 540. Isolation Forest reaches
16.34\%/5.09\% and 18.95\%/5.35\%, respectively; stack kNN improves in every
family.

This gain is budget-dependent: all detector/representation arms capture only
0.29--3.42\% at nominal 0.1--1\% budgets. LOF is weak, and control one-class
SVM removes 21.74\% of held-out unmodified rows at the nominal 5\% budget.
Thus label-free scores recover signal discarded by fixed OPTICS, but do not
solve strict-budget detection. Detectors were prespecified and are all
reported; this discovery result does not establish independent generalization.

\section{Discussion}
The evidence forms a four-level evaluation framework: reproduction,
representation, cluster separability, and label-free operation. The supplied
pipeline is partly reproducible; the stack passes its representation objective;
fixed OPTICS fails at separation; and clean-calibrated detectors recover
moderate absolute signal without a general 540-feature advantage. Success at
one level does not establish the next, and random-row scores do not establish
identity-safe generalization.

The supplied $1\times1500$ implementation and controlled $30\times50$
reconstruction are different experimental objects. The former exposes
constant $H_1$ features, batch-fit confounding, and duplicate leakage. The
latter localizes 97.48\% of collision mass upstream and closes most
single-threshold matches, yet fixed OPTICS lacks poison-dominant density
support. Fewer collisions prove the targeted blind spot was reduced, not that
poison became separable. Clean-calibrated capture identifies fixed OPTICS as a
downstream bottleneck, while confirmation shows that reducing exact collisions
does not reliably improve a different detector.

The detector experiments refine rather than reverse the poster conclusion.
Discovery showed that changing the decision rule can recover more poison than
fixed OPTICS, but confirmation separates that detector gain from a stack gain:
both detectors reverse from positive mean effects on unseen-seed UNSW to
significantly negative effects on matched CICIDS. Moreover, discovery-to-
confirmation Isolation Forest capture falls by 2.35 points for the control and
4.27 for the stack under the same population construction, exposing seed-set
sensitivity and showing why discovery means are not stable estimates. Thus a
label-free rule can use the remaining signal, but multithreshold collision
repair is not a general route to better removal. No downstream classifier
benefit is claimed.

An exploratory seed-42 diagnostic provides indicative mechanism context, not
five-seed evidence. Across 200 source pairs per family, median attack
displacement is 10.7--17.6\% of the median cyclic next-pair unmodified
displacement under the control and 7.0--12.2\% under the stack; no attack
displacement exceeds the corresponding unmodified-pair 95th percentile. This
reference is population-scale, not the local-neighborhood scale governing
OPTICS reachability, so it cannot by itself explain clusterability. The
diagnostic therefore does not have the evidential status of the primary
five-seed findings.

Limitations include incomplete author poison data/settings, a single-seed
18-poison fit diagnostic, and two packet-level derivatives rather than original
flow tables. CICIDS scale testing covers only transpositions; the other families
are matched-size. \emph{Unmodified} includes attack-category traffic, novelty
calibration assumes trusted data, and downstream benefit after removal remains
untested.

\section{Conclusion}
The supplied materials support forensic reconstruction, not exact
reproduction. They expose constant features, incompatible batch fits, and
duplicate-sensitive classifier scores. In the controlled reconstruction, the
nine-threshold stack removes 92.0--95.9\% of exact poison--unmodified matches,
but fixed OPTICS removes at most 0.64\% at strict purity budgets; at
$q>0.50$, matched-cost differences are negative for all four families.

Clean-calibrated discovery scores capture 14.02--18.95\% near 5\% realized
unmodified removal. Independent confirmation retains 7.01--14.73\% absolute
capture, but Isolation Forest stack effects are $+0.69$, $-1.01$, and $+0.50$
points across unseen-seed UNSW, matched CICIDS, and CICIDS scale, with the first
interval crossing zero and the scale clean-cost gate failing; kNN is also
mixed. The remaining signal supports label-free triage, but the representation
repair does not generalize as an operational improvement. Future claims should
preserve provenance, use identity-safe splits, and report capture with realized
unmodified-data cost.
