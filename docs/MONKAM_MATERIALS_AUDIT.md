## Executive assessment

The attachments materially improve the provenance record, but they do not
constitute a complete reproduction package for the published poisoning study.
They establish that the working experimental representation was a `1 x 1500`
payload, that the principal poisoning notebooks used threshold `0.3`, and that
72- and 126-feature variants were produced. They also supply a full 280-feature
UNSW workbook, a 31,000-row 126-feature unmodified-data workbook, and 18 final
poison payloads.

They do **not** supply the complete 1,000-poison set used in the saved notebook
outputs, the adversarial-example generator, the CICIDS2017 payload data, or
chosen HDBSCAN/OPTICS/Mean Shift settings. Only DBSCAN is instantiated in the
notebooks. The material therefore cannot yet reproduce the paper's four-
algorithm poison-separation tables.

The most important methodological issue is that the poisoned and unmodified
126-feature tables were generated by separate calls to `fit_transform` and then
concatenated. `gtda.images.Binarizer` learns the batch maximum at fit time, and
`gtda.diagrams.Scaler` learns a batch-specific diagram scale. This means poison
and unmodified rows were placed in coordinate systems fitted to different
populations before clustering. On the files supplied here, clean payload bytes
reach 255 while all 18 poison examples reach only 121 or 122; at threshold 0.3,
separate fitting can therefore induce effective byte cuts of approximately
76.5 and 36.6 before the second, diagram-scaling difference is even applied.
The saved high DBSCAN silhouette score cannot be treated as evidence of
poison-specific topology until a shared-fit control is run.

### Controlled follow-up completed on 2026-08-31

A preregistered seed-60 diagnostic used all 18 delivered poison examples in a
576-row miniature preserving the supplied notebook's unmodified composition and
3.125% poison share. Separate fitting changed all 18 poison feature vectors and
none of the 558 unmodified vectors; the mean poison-vector L2 displacement was
531.500. Nevertheless, fixed DBSCAN assignments were identical to the shared-
fit arm (ARI 1.0), no poison was removed in either arm, and the silhouette
difference was only 0.0412. The representation-level confound is real, but its
downstream clustering effect was not confirmed in the available 18-poison
diagnostic. The preregistered multi-seed gate therefore remained closed. See
`docs/MONKAM_126_FIT_PROTOCOL_PILOT.md`.

## Notebook findings

### `A_poisoned_converted.ipynb`

- Reads a directory named `poisoned_payload`, transposes each normalized CSV,
  drops the comment/header column, multiplies by 255, and rounds to byte values.
- Saved outputs show 1,000 input rows, although only 18 poison CSVs were
  delivered.
- Builds a 126-feature pipeline with seven filtrations and a 72-feature pipeline
  with six filtrations. Both use `1 x 1500` inputs and threshold 0.3.
- Fits `Binarizer` and `Scaler` on the poison batch alone.
- Converts already-generated adversarial examples; it contains no attack-
  generation procedure and does not identify which delivered example came from
  which attack family.

### `A_Non_poisoned.ipynb`

- Selects all rows 50,138 through 71,137, which are exactly the 21,000 `normal`
  rows, plus 10,000 randomly sampled rows outside that block. Every outside row
  is an attack-category row. The resulting object named `X_begnin` is therefore
  21,000 normal plus 10,000 unmodified malicious rows, not 31,000 benign rows.
- The 10,000-row sample has no `random_state`, so its membership is not
  reproducible from the notebook.
- Fits the same 126-feature pipeline independently on those 31,000 rows, then
  concatenates a separately produced 1,000-row poison feature table.
- The resulting poison share is 3.125% (1,000 of 32,000), not 10%.
- Saved DBSCAN results are:
  - combined half-sample: `eps=200`, `min_samples=500`, silhouette 0.887854,
    labels `[-1, 0, 1]`;
  - unmodified half-sample: `eps=11`, `min_samples=100`, silhouette 0.561074,
    labels `[-1, 0, 1, 2]`.
- No cluster composition table is saved, so the silhouette score alone does not
  establish poison capture or clean-data cost.

### `A_tda_features_extraction.ipynb`

- Mixes several exploratory pipelines and has stale/out-of-order execution.
- Contains a 280-feature configuration, a different 60-feature execution, and
  raw-payload and TDA classifier comparisons.
- Saved random-forest test scores are approximately 0.7922--0.7926 for 280 TDA
  features versus 0.8420 for raw bytes on the 10-class task, and 0.9581 versus
  0.9920 on the saved binary task. These are random row splits, not
  duplicate-group splits.
- A DBSCAN run with `eps=250`, `min_samples=5000` operates on raw payload bytes,
  not on the TDA workbook; its saved silhouette is -0.0370.
- The adjusted Rand index of 0.7020 comes from a synthetic `make_blobs` example
  and is unrelated to UNSW or poisoning.

### Exploratory overview notebooks

- `tda_payload 1.ipynb` and `A_tda_overview_stage1.ipynb` are interactive UNSW
  visualization notebooks. They use unseeded sampling and do not provide the
  published clustering configurations.
- `A_tda_overview_CICIDS2017.ipynb` requires a missing
  `Payload_data_CICIDS2017.csv`; later cells also reference UNSW-only variables.
  It cannot run as supplied.
- Saved notebook state includes one error in `A_tda_features_extraction`, two in
  `A_tda_overview_stage1`, and five in the CICIDS overview. These errors are
  another reason to treat the notebooks as development records, not a clean
  execution package.

Across all six notebooks there are no HDBSCAN, OPTICS, Mean Shift, or bandwidth-
estimation calls. The published article provides search ranges, but neither the
article nor these files supplies the selected settings for those algorithms.

## Data findings

### Delivered poison examples

- There are 18 files representing 11 distinct source-row indices; every source
  row is labeled `dos`.
- None is an exact source copy, and none preserves the source byte multiset.
  Consequently, they are not instances of the multiset-preserving permutation
  families used in the current controlled Frame A/Frame B study.
- The final examples have either 602 nonzero bytes (12 files) or 675 nonzero
  bytes (6 files). They differ from their named source in 595--1,453 positions.
- Multiple variants of the same source differ from one another, indicating
  stochastic generation, but the optimizer, objective, constraints, seed, and
  attack-family identity are absent.
- The ZIP archive adds no new observations: all 18 members exactly match their
  extracted peers.

## Implications for the current codebase

1. `programs/tda_pipeline.py` is not a faithful implementation of the supplied
   Monkam pipeline. It reshapes to `30 x 50`, scales radial centers to that
   raster, and currently yields 60 features. The original paper labels `30 x
   50` as an illustration, while the supplied experimental notebooks use `1 x
   1500` and yield 72/126 features.
2. `programs/clustering.py` defaults are locally chosen values, not settings
   recovered from the paper or attachments. Its module description should not
   say that those parameters are “specified in the paper.”
3. `programs/iterative_filter.py` refits the TDA map on every residual. This
   changes the Binarizer maximum and Scaler state across iterations, so feature
   coordinates drift. It also runs all four clusterers even when only one is
   selected.
4. The iterative implementation uses `is_poisoned` to label clusters Red or
   Green and then uses those oracle labels to decide which rows to remove. It is
   a retrospective upper-bound evaluation, not a deployable sanitizer.
5. `programs/classifier_eval.py` inherits that oracle removal and therefore
   cannot yet demonstrate an operational defense. It also does not persist a
   complete result artifact.
6. The current permutation-family experiments and the 18 delivered poison
   examples answer different threat-model questions. Neither should be
   presented as a direct reproduction of the other.

## Recommended scope for the paper

The strongest defensible paper is now a two-part study:

1. **Forensic reproduction audit of the authors' supplied artifacts.** Report
   what is recoverable, identify the separate-fit preprocessing confound,
   quantify raw and representation collisions in the 280-feature workbook, and
   document what remains missing for exact reproduction.
2. **Controlled mechanism study.** Retain the current Frame A/Frame B result as
   a deliberately controlled 30 x 50, 60-feature experiment showing that a
   multi-threshold representation closes most exact-coordinate collisions but
   does not make OPTICS remove appreciable poison at matched clean-data cost.

The existing Frame A/Frame B numerical findings are not invalidated by the new
files. Their interpretation must be narrowed: they are evidence about the
repository's controlled reconstruction, not about the exact Monkam
implementation or the delivered DoS adversarial examples.

Iterative removal should be the next prospective experiment, not a result
claimed from the current implementation. It should have two explicitly
separated versions:

- an oracle upper bound that uses poison truth only for retrospective cluster
  labeling; and
- a deployable rule based on information available at inference time, such as a
  trusted clean calibration set or a prespecified anomaly/density criterion.

For each iteration, record the parameters, original and residual population,
clean and poisoned counts removed, mixed-cluster evolution, duplicate/collision
composition, and downstream classifier effects. A frozen TDA feature map should
be the primary design; refitting after each removal can be a separately labeled
sensitivity experiment.

## Manuscript changes requiring approval

1. Replace “legacy control” language for the 30 x 50 pipeline with “controlled
   2D reconstruction” or equivalent throughout the abstract, methods, results,
   and conclusion.
2. Add a materials/provenance subsection describing the correspondence,
   supplied file inventory, what was and was not recoverable, and the threshold
   0.3 versus Algorithm 1 threshold 0.4 inconsistency.
3. Add a reproducibility-audit result subsection for separate-fit leakage, the
   misleading `begnin` name, the 3.125% poison share, missing cluster
   configurations, and notebook execution limitations.
4. Add the 280-feature collision and constant-feature findings with exact
   denominator definitions. Do not replace the Frame A 50.80% number with the
   80.587% legacy-workbook share; report them as different frames.
5. Reframe the research questions so the paper distinguishes exact
   reproduction, forensic audit, controlled intervention, and prospective
   iterative re-clustering.
6. State explicitly that the current Frame A/B attacks preserve byte multisets,
   whereas the delivered DoS examples do not.
7. Add a duplicate-group-aware evaluation requirement. Random row splits can
   leak exact raw or feature duplicates across training and test sets.
8. Move any operational “sanitization” or classifier-benefit language behind a
   label-free removal rule and held-out downstream evaluation.
9. Expand limitations to cover incomplete poison/CICIDS materials, unknown
   author seeds and chosen clustering settings, data-dependent transformer
   fitting, 1D H1 degeneracy, and the difference between unmodified and benign.
10. Add an artifact table mapping every result to its dataset, attack family,
    feature map, fit protocol, clustering settings, seeds, and output file.

## Highest-priority next experiment

Before implementing the full iterative study, run a one-variable causal check
on the 126-feature UNSW setup:

1. hold the exact rows, poison examples, threshold, features, DBSCAN parameters,
   and split fixed;
2. compare the notebook's separately fitted poison/unmodified pipelines with a
   single pipeline fitted once on the combined population;
3. record effective Binarizer cuts, Scaler states, cluster composition,
   silhouette, poison capture, and clean false-removal;
4. repeat across fixed seeds only after the single-seed mechanism is verified.

This directly tests whether the reported separation is topological signal or a
batch-fitting artifact. A full run requires the missing 1,000 poison examples;
the 18 supplied files can support only a diagnostic pilot.
